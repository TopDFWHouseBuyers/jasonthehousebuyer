What to Do With an Inherited House in Houston You Do Not Want

Published September 19, 2026 · Houston, Texas
Back to All Articles

Inheriting property in Houston can feel overwhelming, especially when you don't want to keep it. Between probate complications, maintenance costs, and property taxes, an unwanted inherited house can quickly become a burden rather than a blessing.

Understand Your Legal Obligations First

Before making any decisions about your inherited house in Houston, you need to understand the probate process in Texas. The property must typically go through probate court before you can legally sell it. This process can take several months, but once complete, you'll have clear title to proceed. If multiple heirs are involved, all parties must agree on what to do with the property. Consider consulting with a Texas probate attorney to ensure you're following proper procedures and understand any tax implications.

Evaluate the True Cost of Keeping the Property

Many people don't realize how expensive holding onto an inherited house can be. In Houston, you'll face annual property taxes that can range from thousands to tens of thousands depending on the home's value and location. Homeowner's insurance, utilities, HOA fees if applicable, and ongoing maintenance add up quickly. If the house sits vacant, you risk vandalism, plumbing issues, roof damage, and other problems that can cost even more. Calculate these expenses honestly before deciding whether keeping the property makes financial sense.

Your Options for an Unwanted Inherited House

You have several paths forward with your inherited Houston property. You can sell it through a traditional real estate agent, though this requires repairs, staging, and potentially months on the market. Renting it out generates income but makes you a landlord with tenant issues and maintenance responsibilities. You could move in yourself if you need housing. Or you can sell directly to a cash buyer who purchases as-is, eliminating repair costs and closing quickly. Each option has pros and cons depending on your timeline, financial situation, and stress tolerance.

Why Selling for Cash Makes Sense

For many Houston residents with an inherited house they don't want, selling to a cash buyer offers the fastest relief. You won't need to invest money in repairs or updates. There are no agent commissions eating into your proceeds. You can close in as little as seven days or on your timeline. Consider the Emotional Side of Inheritance

An inherited house often comes with emotional weight, especially if it belonged to a parent or close relative. You might feel guilty about selling a family home or pressured by other family members to keep it. Remember that making a practical decision doesn't diminish your memories or love for the person who left you the property. Sometimes the kindest thing you can do for yourself is to sell and use the proceeds in a way that honors their memory while supporting your own financial wellbeing.
